**Jensen Huang, CEO**
[08:40] Thank you, Colette. Good afternoon, everyone.

The age of AI is in full force. Enterprises, sovereign nations, hyperscalers, and startups around the world are racing to build AI infrastructure, and demand for NVIDIA's platforms is extraordinary.

Let me walk you through what's happening. Blackwell is in full production. We shipped Blackwell-based systems to all major cloud providers — AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle Cloud. We also shipped to a new and growing class of GPU cloud providers: CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, Crusoe, and others. These GPU cloud providers have emerged as an important new segment. They exist because demand from enterprises and AI labs for on-demand GPU capacity exceeds what the hyperscalers can provision today.

Blackwell is the world's most powerful chip for AI inference. A single GB200 NVL72 rack — 72 GPUs connected with NVLink — delivers 30x the inference performance of our previous Hopper architecture for large language models. For reasoning models that require chain-of-thought computation — models like OpenAI's o1, o3, DeepSeek R1, and others — Blackwell's efficiency advantage is even more dramatic. Inference is the new workload. It's compute-intensive, latency-sensitive, and scales with demand.

The Blackwell ramp was our fastest product generation launch ever. We exceeded $11 billion in Blackwell revenue this quarter alone, compared to zero revenue two quarters ago.

Let me talk about inference demand specifically. The world is moving from training to inference at enormous scale. Every major AI lab and every major enterprise is now deploying AI agents — software programs that reason, plan, and take actions autonomously. These agents are inference-heavy. They call models hundreds or thousands of times to complete a task. The demand for inference compute is going to grow dramatically from here.

DeepSeek was an interesting moment in January. It demonstrated that open-source Chinese AI labs had made remarkable progress with efficient training. Markets initially reacted with concern. But the correct interpretation is the opposite: DeepSeek showed that AI inference demand is scaling faster than anyone realized. More efficient training means more models, more applications, and more inference. We saw no softening in orders.

Sovereign AI is accelerating. Countries around the world — India, Japan, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, France, Italy — are all making large-scale investments in national AI infrastructure. They want to own their AI data, train on their own languages and cultures, and capture the economic value of AI domestically. NVIDIA is the platform of choice for these sovereign AI initiatives.

Our networking business — InfiniBand and Ethernet for AI — continues to grow rapidly. The largest AI clusters require hundreds of thousands of GPUs connected at massive bandwidth. Spectrum-X, our Ethernet for AI networking platform, is seeing strong adoption. We shipped over a million networking ports this quarter.

NVIDIA Inference Microservices, or NIMs — our pre-packaged AI model containers — now number over 500 models. NIMs make it easy for enterprises to deploy AI. We're seeing rapid enterprise adoption across healthcare, financial services, manufacturing, and retail.

On the automotive front, our DRIVE Orin and DRIVE Thor platforms are winning. BYD — the world's largest EV maker — adopted DRIVE Thor for their next generation of intelligent vehicles. We're also powering robotaxi fleets: Waymo, WeRide, and others are scaling on NVIDIA platforms.

Robotics is an emerging opportunity. We launched Isaac GR00T — our foundation model for humanoid robots — and it's being adopted by leading robotics companies. The physical AI era is beginning.

I want to address a question we're getting often: what about the data center infrastructure cycle? Is this a bubble? Is demand durable?

Let me be direct. We are at the beginning. The global installed base of data center infrastructure needs to be rebuilt from CPU-centric to GPU-accelerated. There are roughly $1 trillion worth of installed data center infrastructure globally. Nearly all of it was built for general-purpose computing. AI computing requires GPU acceleration. The transition will take years and is not optional — companies that don't deploy AI will be outcompeted.

The companies driving demand — the hyperscalers — have been explicit: Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta have collectively guided to over $300 billion in capex in 2025. A large portion of that is going to AI. This isn't speculative; it's contracted and in process.

I am incredibly proud of the NVIDIA team. What we've built — the full-stack NVIDIA AI platform from silicon to software to services — is unique. No one else has this. Competitors are years behind, and the moat gets wider every quarter.

**Analyst (Stacy Rasgon, Bernstein)**
[45:30] Jensen, you mentioned DeepSeek. Can you speak more specifically about what you've seen in terms of order patterns and demand signals since the DeepSeek news? Have you seen any changes in customer conversations?

**Jensen Huang**
[45:48] No change in demand signals whatsoever. If anything, interest increased. Let me explain why.

DeepSeek demonstrated a more efficient training methodology using fewer GPUs with chain-of-thought reasoning and reinforcement learning from human feedback. That's impressive. But what it also demonstrated is that this approach — long chain-of-thought reasoning — is inference-intensive. You're doing many more tokens of computation per user query. This is sometimes called "test-time compute scaling." The better the model reasons, the more compute it uses at inference. And so the net effect of models like DeepSeek R1 is that inference demand goes up, not down.

Additionally, if training becomes cheaper, more models get trained. More models means more applications, more use cases, more inference. You see this logic: cheaper and more accessible AI means more AI — not less. Jevons paradox applies perfectly here.

**Analyst (Timothy Arcuri, UBS)**
[52:10] Jensen, can you talk about the hyperscaler concentration risk? Your top three customers are a significant portion of revenue. Is there any risk they start deploying their own custom chips and reducing NVIDIA spending?

**Jensen Huang**
[52:25] Our hyperscaler customers are among the most sophisticated technology organizations on the planet. They absolutely design their own custom chips — Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium, Microsoft's Maia. I think this is great. It reflects the extraordinary importance of AI. And yet, all of them continue to significantly grow their NVIDIA GPU deployments.

Why? Because the use cases for GPUs are different from TPUs and ASICs. Custom chips are optimized for specific workloads — they're great at training a specific model architecture at extreme scale. GPUs are general-purpose and flexible. A GPU can run any model, train any architecture, do inference of any kind. When a new model comes out — a new reasoning model, a new multimodal model — you can deploy it on NVIDIA GPUs immediately. Custom chips require months of redesign.

In a world where AI is advancing rapidly, flexibility has enormous value. So I expect our hyperscaler customers to have a heterogeneous mix of NVIDIA GPUs and their own custom silicon — but NVIDIA's share of their AI spend will remain significant.
